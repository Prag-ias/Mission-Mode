## Rationale, per topic

### SNT-15 — Diversity of life — microbes, fungi, plant and animal groups, behaviour and world fauna

**Covers:** 2021 Q20, 2021 Q21, 2021 Q26, 2022 Q38, 2022 Q99, 2023 Q12, 2023 Q15, 2023 Q16, 2023 Q18, 2023 Q19, 2024 Q19, 2024 Q23, 2024 Q29

The single largest genuinely unowned area in the whole tree, and it was proposed twice — ENV-17 and SNT-15 are the same four NCERT chapters under two names. Kept in SNT because SNT-01's source_ref is already 'NCERT 11–12 Bio selected', so this completes a book the candidate opens anyway and sits next to it in phase 2; ENV is an all-Shankar subject where a lone NCERT-Bio node would read as an import. Scope is the union of both proposals: biological classification and microbes (bacteria, viruses, extremophiles, probiotics), fungi and lichens, algae/bryophytes/pteridophytes and plant families, animal phyla and body plans, and the natural-history limb — adaptations, defence and toxins, behaviour, which species lives where. This relieves SNT-01, which the tagger had loaded with 13 organismal questions it is not named for, and leaves ENV-07 as the Indian-species shelf. 150 min matches SNT-05 and SNT-09 and reflects the exam-relevant slice, not a full taxonomy read. Rate is roughly two questions a paper — the highest-yield addition here by a wide margin. 2021 Q21 (economic botany) and 2022 Q99 (DNA barcoding) are claimed as shared rather than as justification.

### SNT-16 — Armed forces organisation — services, ranks, commands, CDS and named operations

**Covers:** 2024 Q69, 2024 Q77, 2024 Q78, 2026 Q73

SNT-07 is defence technology — missiles, aircraft, DRDO, stealth — and teaches none of what these four ask: rank equivalence across the three services, the CDS as head of the Department of Military Affairs, named domestic Army operations, and Corps/command headquarters. Nothing else in 186 topics covers force structure. What lifts it above the one-question rejects is the trend: all four fall in 2024 and 2026, the two most recent papers. Two changes from the proposal. The CAPF/Home Guards limb is removed and given to POL-29, which owns the police as a State List subject — that was the only real overlap between the SNT and POL proposals, and with it gone the topic is a bounded list (service structure, commands, rank tables, higher defence organisation, operation names), so 75 min is right, matching SNT-06 and SNT-11 rather than SNT-14's 90. Source changed from Ashok Kumar's Internal Security to PT365 plus the MoD annual report: buying a Mains-oriented book for 75 minutes of rank tables is not a trade worth making. intro_phase 3 follows SNT-14 — the operation-names half firms up on the PT365 cycle.

### POL-29 — Criminal justice and internal security — BNS/BNSS/BSA, police, prisons, CAPFs, security laws

**Covers:** 2021 Q83, 2023 Q32, 2023 Q77, 2023 Q91, 2023 Q92, 2026 Q62

The tree runs from the Constitution down to the courts at POL-20 and then stops; nothing covers the machinery underneath. Six gap questions fall in this one hole — prisons and parole (2021 Q83, 2023 Q32), anti-terror law (2023 Q77 stmt 3), police organisation and Home Guards (2023 Q91), miscellaneous criminal statutes (2023 Q92) and the new criminal codes (2026 Q62). POL-27 Governance was the placeholder for four of them and teaches RTI, e-governance and civil services, with no police or prisons content at all. Kept as one topic, not split into criminal law and internal security: four of the six are from 2023 alone, so the honest rate is about one a year and two topics would buy ten scheduled revisions for 1.2% of the paper. Now also carries the CAPFs and Home Guards, taken from the SNT proposal, which keeps every paramilitary and police force on one shelf under the State List frame while SNT-16 keeps the three services. Prelims-sized scope: what changed in BNS/BNSS/BSA versus IPC/CrPC/Evidence Act, Zero FIR and arrest procedure, police and CAPFs, the Model Prisons Act 2023, and the named security statutes. 120 min matches POL-27 and POL-22. Phase 2 follows POL-27: it needs Art 21/22 and the State List read first, and its content churns. Medium not high — the 2023 spike is one year — but the codes came into force in 2024 and 2026 already tested BNSS, so 2027 will almost certainly ask something.

### CAI-11 — International organisations — UN agencies, funds and programmes; non-UN bodies (IUCN, WEF, ICRC)

**Covers:** 2021 Q50, 2023 Q13, 2023 Q30, 2024 Q24, 2024 Q26, 2026 Q38

The signature of a genuinely missing node: the same question type landed in a different bucket every year — CAI-01 in 2021, CAI-04 in 2023 and 2026, CAI-07 in 2024, and from the environment side ENV-05 in 2023 and ENV-15 in 2024. The recurring hook is the institution itself: is this body inside the UN system, what is its mandate, what does it publish. Absorbs the ENV specialist's ENV-18 proposal entirely — that topic's two questions (2023 Q13, 2024 Q26) were already in this one's covers list, and an international-bodies roster belongs in CAI, not in a Shankar-sourced ENV subject; ENV-10 stays Indian institutions by design. Seams held deliberately tight so it does not sprawl: CAI-04 keeps the UN as a political forum and India's groupings, CAI-08 keeps reports and indices (outputs, not bodies), and this topic takes the agency roster and the non-UN bodies. 90 min matches CAI-05, CAI-07 and CAI-08 — one read clears about six of 500 questions, roughly one a year, every year, and it is pure recall so it pays back cheaply. Weight set to medium rather than the proposed high: one a year is a real pattern but not GEO-24 or SNT-15 territory.

### GEO-25 — India — water resources: availability, groundwater, water stress, conservation

**Covers:** 2023 Q66, 2023 Q76

The cleanest structural hole in the set, and verifiable rather than inferred: GEO maps NCERT 12 'India — People and Economy' chapter by chapter — GEO-17 is Ch.5, GEO-18 is Ch.7, GEO-19 is Ch.8, GEO-20 is Ch.10–11 — and Chapter 6, Water Resources, is silently skipped. Nothing teaches surface versus groundwater availability, the per-capita water budget, groundwater extraction and depletion, water-stressed basins, or water conservation and the National Water Policy. GEO-13 teaches rivers as drainage, not as a resource; GEO-17 teaches irrigation as an agricultural input, which is why the groundwater-withdrawal question was dumped there at medium confidence. This is the addition that most 'feels like it was always part of it' — it restores a chapter the tree already implies. 90 min is one NCERT chapter, the same as GEO-18, GEO-19 and GEO-20; phase 2 matches every India economic-geography topic. Medium, not high: two direct hits in 600 questions plus adjacency to 2023 Q4 and 2026 Q28.

### ANM-15 — Sources, texts and the scientific legacy of ancient India

**Covers:** 2021 Q40, 2024 Q64, 2026 Q7

ANM-01 to ANM-09 are purely dynastic-political. Nothing teaches the non-political layer every ancient-India book opens and closes with: how we know (inscriptions, coins, archaeology, foreign travellers from Megasthenes to Fa-Hien and Hiuen Tsang), what the texts are (Vedic corpus, Sutras, Smritis and their commentaries including Mitakshara and Dayabhaga, the Buddhist and Jain canons, Sangam and classical Sanskrit), and the knowledge legacy (place-value and zero, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Varahamihira, Charaka, Sushruta, metallurgy). Three gap questions land here and fit nowhere else — 2021 Q40 was parked on ANM-10 on chronology alone, 2026 Q7 on ANM-08 on date alone. It is one chapter-shaped reading unit, not a merge of three unrelated things, and UPSC draws on it most years. ART-10 is not a substitute: it is Singhania's culture chapters already carrying literature, philosophy, UNESCO and GI tags in 120 minutes, and teaches none of the source-criticism or the science. 120 min matches ANM-01, ANM-02, ANM-05 and ANM-08; phase 2 matches all of ANM. Source names book and section rather than inventing chapter numbers, following the 'Laxmikanth Governance section' precedent.

## Merged across subjects

- ENV-17 'Diversity of life' merged into SNT-15. The same body of reading — NCERT 11 Biology Ch.1–4 — was proposed twice at 150 minutes each, and the two covers lists overlapped on the behaviour questions (2023 Q15, Q19). SNT wins the content: SNT-01's source is already 'NCERT 11–12 Bio selected', so the new topic completes a book that is opened anyway and schedules beside it in phase 2, whereas ENV is entirely Shankar-sourced and a lone NCERT-Bio node there would read as an import. SNT-15's name and scope widened to carry ENV-17's natural-history limb (behaviour, adaptations, world fauna) so nothing is lost in the merge. Saves 150 minutes and five scheduled revisions.
- ENV-18 'Global environmental bodies' merged into CAI-11. Both of ENV-18's questions (2023 Q13 IUCN/UNEP/WWF, 2024 Q26 WEF) were already in CAI-11's covers list, and CAI-11 already named IUCN and WEF in its own scope — the ENV specialist and the CAI specialist were looking at the same missing shelf from two sides. An international-organisations roster belongs in CAI; ENV-10 stays Indian institutions by design. Saves 75 minutes.
- SNT-16's CAPF, paramilitary and Home Guards limb merged into POL-29. Both proposals claimed the CAPFs, the only genuine scope collision between the SNT and POL trees. The seam now runs where the constitutional line does: POL-29 takes the police as a State List subject with the CAPFs and Home Guards above it, SNT-16 keeps the three armed services, ranks, commands, CDS and named operations. This is why SNT-16 comes down from 90 minutes to 75.

## Dropped

- CAI-12 'Defence diplomacy and international security bodies' (75 min) — two questions in six years, and its own author offered to fold it if SNT or POL proposed a domestic defence topic, which SNT-16 is. Both questions sit on shelves the tagger called correct rather than nearest: 2024 Q82 Mitra Shakti is India–Sri Lanka, so CAI-01 neighbourhood is the right bucket, and 2026 Q76 INTERPOL notices is CAI-07 global governance. The bounded lists it wanted to teach — exercise names and partners, FATF grey list, IAEA safeguards, NSG/MTCR — are each a paragraph inside CAI-01/02/03 and CAI-07, which are read anyway.
- ECO-21 'Labour, employment and social security' (90 min) — one question in six years (2021 Q2). The labour-codes gap is real and ECO-17 does teach measurement rather than labour law, but a first read plus five scheduled revisions cannot be bought on a single PYQ. ECO-17 (Mrunal Pillar 5) stays the shelf; the gig-worker limb is already in ECO-20. The most likely of the three ECO proposals to be tested in 2027, and still not enough.
- ECO-22 'Micro-economics basics' (90 min) — one question (2021 Q4), and the proposal's own pyq_weight is low. It costs a whole NCERT read for price theory that Prelims rarely asks directly, and the vocabulary that matters downstream (elasticity in the MSP and subsidy context, market structure in industrial policy) is picked up inside ECO-09, ECO-14 and ECO-15. ECO-01 remains the general-concepts bucket.
- ECO-23 'Insurance and pension sector' (90 min) — one question (2026 Q96), and half of that is the Montreal Convention on aviation liability. The structural argument is the best of the three — a financial-sector spine with banking, NBFCs and capital markets but no insurance — but Mrunal Pillar 1D is already cited by ECO-07 and ECO-08, so the IRDAI, LIC, PFRDA and NPS pages are read regardless. What is missing is a shelf and a revision slot, not the reading, and one question does not buy those.
- ANM-16 'Regional kingdoms of the 15th century' (90 min) — one question (2023 Q49), pyq_weight low, and its author flagged it as the weaker of his two proposals and named the cheaper alternative himself: widen ANM-12 from 'Vijayanagara and the Bahmani kingdoms' to include the regional kingdoms and add about 60 minutes there rather than creating a fifteenth ANM node. Take the widening, not the topic. ANM stops at ANM-15 so no numbering hole is left.

## Open question: study time

Adding a topic fixes tagging and makes the coverage grid honest. It does **not** allocate study
time — `generate-plan.mjs` built the 912 blocks from the old tree, so a new topic sits permanently
unread unless the plan is re-run with `--from`. That is a separate decision about the next eight months.
